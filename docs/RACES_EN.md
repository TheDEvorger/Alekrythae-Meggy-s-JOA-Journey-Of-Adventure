# 🌌 The Peoples of Ałek’ryŧhæ
## Canonical Peoples Encyclopedia · World Lore Compendium

> **A people is known not only by appearance, but by the lands they inhabit, the senses they carry, the cities they build, and the mark they leave upon the world.**

This document is a lore reference for the **24 canonical peoples** used by **Ałek’ryŧhæ · Journey of Adventurer + Tavern**.

The names presented here are the **original canonical names used in the game**. Familiar terms such as “human,” “dragonkin,” “ice elf,” or “werewolf” appear only as quick bridges for fantasy readers. They never replace the canonical name.

Every people in Ałek’ryŧhæ possesses a unique **Primary Instinctive Root**. This sense does not replace ordinary sight, hearing, or smell. It is an additional biological or A̤ɐ͜ɨǣ́ꞎ͡ƣ-based layer through which the body interprets the world. True hybrids may inherit more than one root.

> **Every people is defined not only by appearance, but by how they perceive the world, inhabit it, build within it, and shape its history.**

# I · Lands, Cities, and Ancient Lineages

## 01 · 🏛️ Ħǽɱ’Ŧʏǽɍŋ
> *The Most Adaptable People*

**Fantasy-reader bridge:** Human  
**Common homelands:** Kingdoms, cities, villages, ports, farms, mines, courts, armies, academies, and trade roads  
**Primary instinct:** **Adaptive Sense**  
**Guiding idea:** *Survive.*

### Identity
Ħǽɱ’Ŧʏǽɍŋ are the most widespread and variable people of the world. Where many other peoples are born into powerful environmental or biological specializations, Ħǽɱ’Ŧʏǽɍŋ derive their strength from their ability to **adapt**. A dockworker, mountain farmer, court scholar, mercenary, monk, trader, and nomad may all belong to the same people while living radically different lives.

Physically, they are less specialized than many of the setting’s great peoples. Their unusual advantage is compatibility with equipment, potions, accessories, and external enhancements. Preparation therefore carries tremendous cultural and adventuring value.

### Society and place in the world
There is no single Ħǽɱ’Ŧʏǽɍŋ civilization. Devout city-states, mercantile republics, military kingdoms, scholarly centers, and humble rural communities may coexist in the same age. This diversity makes them both the connective tissue of world politics and one of its least predictable forces.

### Instinct and narrative identity
**Adaptive Sense** allows the body to intuit how it may respond to an environment, tool, potion, or enhancement. A Ħǽɱ’Ŧʏǽɍŋ hero is rarely defined by being born the strongest. Their archetypal strength is learning, preparing, improvising, and continuing to grow beyond an expected limit.

## 02 · 🕯️ Ḓɐꞎ’Ɍɨɱ
> *The Dark Nobility*

**Fantasy-reader bridge:** Vampire-like in aesthetic, but not blood-drinking vampires  
**Social structure:** Queen-centered aristocratic monarchy  
**Primary instinct:** **Night-Presence Sense**  
**Guiding idea:** *Order is everything.*

### Identity
Ḓɐꞎ’Ɍɨɱ unite darkness with **discipline, elegance, and measure** rather than decay. Moon-pale complexions, dark or silver hair, severe eyes, formal dress, and cultivated physical grace shape their visual identity. They are not classical vampires and do not feed on blood.

Their natural life class is ageless but killable. That distinction gives memory, tradition, and long political horizons unusual weight in their civilization.

### Society and civilization
The Queen is more than a ruler. She is a symbol of historical continuity and the ideal of ordered society. Palaces, fortresses, arts, ceremonial law, military training, and refined craft all belong to the same cultural fabric. Hierarchy is strong, but in the ideal Ḓɐꞎ’Ɍɨɱ vision, hierarchy exists to preserve stability and prosperity rather than mere domination.

### Instinct and narrative identity
**Night-Presence Sense** detects hidden movement, concealed beings, and irregularities in low light. Ḓɐꞎ’Ɍɨɱ stories thrive on duty, nobility, ancient memory, court politics, and the conflict between inherited law and individual conscience.

## 03 · ♟️ Ɵɍʏ’Ƙǽŋ
> *The People of Will and Conquest*

**Fantasy-reader bridge:** No direct stock-fantasy equivalent  
**Common homelands:** Open cities where living nature and monumental architecture interlock, often under Ʋħǽ’ɍűŋ influence  
**Primary instinct:** **Will-Threshold Sense**

### Identity
Ɵɍʏ’Ƙǽŋ place fearless will, expansion, and imposed order near the center of their culture. The black-violet presence of Ʋħǽ’ɍűŋ can surround their lands, yet those lands are not dead wastelands. Violet, ash, gold, and deep blue coexist with gardens, plazas, markets, and open architecture.

One of their defining traits is their relationship with fear. They can understand fear and recognize it in others, but do not experience it as the same instinctive brake felt by ordinary peoples.

### Society and worldview
Their cities may combine palace, market, arena, barracks, theater, and living garden in a single urban composition. Rather than erasing nature, they often reshape it into the structure of their civilization. Their conquest-oriented worldview therefore produces a culture that can be beautiful, productive, and deeply dangerous at the same time.

### Instinct and narrative identity
**Will-Threshold Sense** reads determination, fear, attack readiness, and disturbances around Ʋħǽ’ɍűŋ thresholds. Ɵɍʏ’Ƙǽŋ keep one of the setting’s central questions alive: when does strength create order, and when does order become domination?

## 04 · 🌊 Ɍʏɨɍʉŋ’Χɐɍűŋ
> *The People of the Water Horizon*

**Fantasy-reader bridge:** Fully aquatic humanoids without fish tails  
**Common homelands:** Freshwater oceans, coastal settlements, half-submerged cities, and fully underwater cities  
**Primary instinct:** **Water-Movement Sense**

### Body and identity
Ɍʏɨɍʉŋ’Χɐɍűŋ retain a two-armed, two-legged humanoid form. What makes them truly aquatic is the adaptation of the entire body: low-friction skin, webbed hands and feet, exceptionally developed swimming musculature, and the **Velyth Breath**, which allows life both above and below the surface.

Their diet is strongly carnivorous and their dentition reflects a predatory ecology. Water is not an obstacle that slows them; it is the medium in which their bodies become fully natural.

### Cities and culture
Underwater settlements may feature mineral arches, luminous algae gardens, heated stone chambers, and public spaces organized around currents. Their ability to move between coast and depth makes them natural partners in maritime trade, rescue, exploration, and deep-water travel.

### Instinct and narrative identity
**Water-Movement Sense** reads current, vibration, displaced water, movement trails, and changes in gravitational direction within water. Canon lore gives trained individuals the potential to reach underwater movement comparable to Old-Earth Mach 1 scales. Their defining fantasy is not command over water, but **perfect belonging within it**.

## 05 · 🐋 Þɐꞎɐśś’Ɱǿɍʏɴ
> *The People of Cloud Roads and Sky Whales*

**Fantasy-reader bridge:** Sky-island people  
**Common homelands:** Floating lands, dense cloud layers, sky cities, and whale routes  
**Primary instinct:** **Cloud-Path Sense**

### World and life
Þɐꞎɐśś’Ɱǿɍʏɴ civilization begins where sky becomes geography. Their floating realms may carry mountains, forests, lakes, farms, rivers, and waterfalls. Dense cloud layers can become temporary roads, camps, or landing grounds rather than mere mist.

They do not possess ordinary true flight. Powerful legs and A̤ɐ͜ɨǣ́ꞎ͡ƣ-adapted musculature allow jumps of roughly **24 meters vertically** under favorable conditions.

### Sky whales
Their most famous companions are enormous domesticated flying whales. Smaller animals may serve as mounts and carriers; the largest can reach roughly **2,400 meters** in length and support mobile settlements upon their backs.

### Instinct and narrative identity
**Cloud-Path Sense** reads cloud density, lift zones, aerial routes, and balance changes in floating masses. Þɐꞎɐśś’Ɱǿɍʏɴ provide a natural home for stories of exploration, living transportation networks, sky trade, vertical geography, and the dangers of a world without ground beneath every road.

## 06 · 🌴 Ɱǽṙɇþɨ’Śɵꞎɐʏŋ
> *The Rhythm People of the Warm Coasts*

**Fantasy-reader bridge:** Tropical island and coastal people  
**Common homelands:** Tropical islands, coral harbors, warm coasts, floating markets  
**Primary instinct:** **Biochemical Rhythm Sense**

### Identity and culture
Ɱǽṙɇþɨ’Śɵꞎɐʏŋ are associated with warm climates, sea life, music, scent, color, trade, and hospitality. Caramel, cocoa, and deep brown complexions, wavy or curled hair, and vivid coastal clothing form part of their recognizable visual character.

They can remain underwater for approximately **24 minutes**, making them exceptional divers, rescuers, pearl gatherers, and wreck salvagers.

### Their relationship with water
Their power is not delicate “water puppetry.” It acts upon the motion of existing water masses, enlarging or redirecting their force. Skilled individuals can shape major waves, while exceptional masters may reach far greater scales when sufficient water is available.

### Instinct and narrative identity
**Biochemical Rhythm Sense** interprets toxins, scents, exertion, stress, oils, resins, and other chemical or rhythmic signs in living bodies and the environment. Their stories can be as much about ports, healing, trade, culture, and navigation as they are about battle.

## 07 · 🍷 Þɨɍ’Ŋɵčŧ
> *The Cursed of Blood Resonance*

**Fantasy-reader bridge:** Cursed vampire-adjacent people  
**Origin:** An ancient curse rooted in Ḓɐꞎ’Ɍɨɱ history  
**Primary instinct:** **Blood-Resonance Sense**

### Identity
Þɨɍ’Ŋɵčŧ resemble a familiar dark-fantasy archetype while resisting the simple “undead vampire” category. Blood is a biological necessity for them. They do not age normally, but they can be killed, making their existence one of prolonged vulnerability rather than effortless immortality.

Their frightening reputation often arrives before the individual. Yet a Þɨɍ’Ŋɵčŧ is a thinking person with memory, will, and moral agency beyond the hunger that defines part of their biology.

### Society and place in the world
Some regions hunt them. Others tolerate tightly controlled arrangements or secret agreements. Their history with Ḓɐꞎ’Ɍɨɱ is more than simple hatred: it carries themes of responsibility, consequence, inherited guilt, and what later generations owe to the victims of older mistakes.

### Instinct and narrative identity
**Blood-Resonance Sense** perceives the presence and changing condition of living blood. Their strongest stories need not be about horror alone. They can center on living with a curse, preserving agency, and resisting the identity that fearful societies assign to them.

## 08 · 🐲 Ḓɍɐỿ’Žűɍχǽɍ
> *The Golden-Winged Dragon Lineage*

**Fantasy-reader bridge:** Dragonkin  
**Common homelands:** High peaks, volcanic ridges, stone citadels, treasure halls  
**Primary instinct:** **Thermal-Flight Sense**

### Identity and appearance
Ḓɍɐỿ’Žűɍχǽɍ carry an unmistakably draconic silhouette: wings, horns, tails, and richly colored scales. Yet they are not miniature dragons. They are a humanoid people with their own institutions, architecture, memory, and craft traditions.

Mountain cities favor broad landing courts, mining terraces, gold-domed halls, silver towers, and corridors aligned with hot mountain winds. Metal, stone, precious materials, and inherited objects carry cultural weight.

### Flight and craft
Flight is more than beating wings. Reading thermals, volcanic updrafts, and fast aerial corridors is a trained art. This makes them uniquely capable travelers and combatants in high mountain and volcanic terrain.

### Instinct and narrative identity
**Thermal-Flight Sense** reads rising hot air, volcanic thermals, and flight vectors. Ḓɍɐỿ’Žűɍχǽɍ are ideal for epic-fantasy stories of lineage, treasure, craft, mountain citadels, and mastery of dangerous skies.

## 09 · 🍃 Ǽþ’Ʋǽɍʏŋ
> *Ancient Guardians of Life*

**Fantasy-reader bridge:** Nature-elf analogue  
**Primary homeland:** Ʋǽꞎ’þɐɍʏŋ  
**Primary instinct:** **Life Sense**  
**Guiding idea:** *Life has value.*

### Identity
Ǽþ’Ʋǽɍʏŋ are an ancient people of grace, long memory, and close relationship with living landscapes. Their connection to forests is not merely aesthetic. They can perceive vitality, sickness, injury, and biological change with a depth unavailable to ordinary senses.

Family, loyalty, memory, and stewardship of living environments are major cultural themes. These values can make them peaceful, but never helpless. When a living region is threatened, they can become disciplined and formidable defenders.

### Civilization
Settlements grow around great trees, waterways, forest corridors, and living architecture. Structures aim to belong to the landscape rather than hide it beneath stone.

### Instinct and narrative identity
**Life Sense** reads health, vitality, and biological traces in the environment. Their stories naturally explore ancient memory, stewardship, the limits of civilization, and the responsibilities of a long-lived people in a changing world.

## 10 · 🔥 Ƶɐṙ’Χɐɍžűŋ
> *The People of Red Heat and Metal Veins*

**Fantasy-reader bridge:** Fire-elf or forge-folk analogue  
**Common homelands:** Hot mining biomes, foundry valleys, furnace cities, metal mountains  
**Primary instinct:** **Heat-Metal Sense**  
**Guiding idea:** *Words cool; work remains.*

### Identity
Ƶɐṙ’Χɐɍžűŋ are not simply “fire users.” They are a people adapted to hot mineral environments, marked by red heat-like lines beneath the skin and an extraordinary relationship with metal as a material.

Their cities live through red stone, steel bridges, furnace towers, casting halls, crimson glass, and subterranean workshops.

### Craft and society
Reputation is earned through work. A reliable bridge, flawless blade, durable vessel, or perfectly cast mechanical joint can speak more loudly than ceremony. Guild tradition and professional responsibility therefore carry enormous cultural weight.

### Instinct and narrative identity
**Heat-Metal Sense** detects temperature distribution, internal stress in metal, ore behavior, and thermal fracture points. Ƶɐṙ’Χɐɍžűŋ bring craft, engineering, trade, and the hidden labor behind great kingdoms into the center of fantasy storytelling.

## 11 · 😼 Ɐʉ’Ƀɇŋ
> *The People of Rooftops, Harbors, and Quiet Sounds*

**Fantasy-reader bridge:** Catfolk  
**Common homelands:** Harbor districts, markets, inns, back streets, rooftops  
**Primary instinct:** **Auditory-Predator Sense**  
**Guiding idea:** *Profit and freedom.*

### Identity
Ɐʉ’Ƀɇŋ are easily recognized by feline ears, tails, sharp eyes, and agile bodies. Curious, mobile, and independent, they are difficult to bind to a single political model. A city’s finest guide, merchant, lookout, courier, or rogue may all be Ɐʉ’Ƀɇŋ.

### Urban culture
Their neighborhoods are layered, noisy, and alive. Rooftop routes, small shops, market networks, inns, and information chains form part of daily life. Instead of centralized empires, they often accumulate influence through trade, reputation, and personal connections.

### Instinct and narrative identity
**Auditory-Predator Sense** separates tiny sounds, direction changes, vibration, footsteps, and breathing. It makes them exceptionally suited to exploration, pursuit, urban adventures, and espionage. To an Ɐʉ’Ƀɇŋ, the world is far more acoustically detailed than most people realize.

## 12 · 💎 Žɨꞎ’Ƙɍɐŧ
> *The Small Masters Who Make Stone Speak*

**Fantasy-reader bridge:** An original subterranean people with goblin, dwarf, and elf echoes  
**Common homelands:** Cave cities, crystal veins, mountain interiors, forest mines  
**Primary instinct:** **Vein-Void Sense**

### Identity
Žɨꞎ’Ƙɍɐŧ are small, green-skinned subterranean folk, often around a meter in height. Unlike the destructive “monster goblin” stereotype, they are known for productivity, community, mining, and craft.

Stone and crystal work are not merely trades. They are central to identity. A Žɨꞎ’Ƙɍɐŧ may recognize an unsafe wall, a hidden void, or the direction of a mineral vein with extraordinary precision.

### Society
Their settlements are scaled to smaller bodies yet may contain intricate mine systems, workshops, crystal halls, and defended passages. They value honest work and mutual goodwill, but can close their roads firmly against exploiters.

### Instinct and narrative identity
**Vein-Void Sense** reads mineral veins, fractures, cave spaces, and underground geometry. Žɨꞎ’Ƙɍɐŧ turn the underground from “a dungeon” into a complete geography, profession, and civilization.

## 13 · 🪓 Χɐʉɍ’Ǥɐþ
> *The Green Amazons*

**Fantasy-reader bridge:** Somewhere between amazon warriors and honorable forest-orc archetypes  
**Common homelands:** Great forests, clan camps, tree dwellings, hunting territories  
**Primary instinct:** **Force Sense**  
**Guiding idea:** *Strength has meaning when it protects what it claims.*

### Identity
Χɐʉɍ’Ǥɐþ are an all-female, green-skinned forest people of great physical power and endurance. Their imposing appearance can seem savage to outsiders, but their culture is not built around senseless violence. Its central themes are **strength, protection, responsibility, and independence**.

### Society and war
Forest camps, tree dwellings, hunting grounds, and defensive routes form the basis of daily life. Physical training is not merely a warrior profession; understanding what the body can do is an important part of maturity. Strength alone does not justify rule over others.

### Instinct and narrative identity
**Force Sense** reads muscle tension, weight transfer, and the direction of physical attacks. In close combat, this makes them dangerous not only because they are powerful, but because they understand how power is moving through an opponent’s body.

## 14 · ⛰️ Ɱʉɵ’Ŋþɨɍ
> *The Great-Massed Free People*

**Fantasy-reader bridge:** Giants  
**Common homelands:** Mountain foothills, broad plains, villages, camps, small fortresses  
**Primary instinct:** **Mass-Structure Sense**  
**Guiding idea:** *Freedom is strength.*

### Identity
Ɱʉɵ’Ŋþɨɍ greatly exceed ordinary peoples in height, mass, and physical power. Heavy loads, large tools, stone construction, and long periods of physical labor can be ordinary parts of life.

Yet their central cultural theme is not sheer size. It is **freedom**. Forced rule, enslavement, and outside control over their lands are among the surest ways to turn a peaceful community into determined opposition.

### Settlements
Rather than endless monumental palaces, many communities favor wide courtyards, strong stonework, mountain roads, and open country. Herding, heavy construction, transport, and border defense fit naturally into social life.

### Instinct and narrative identity
**Mass-Structure Sense** reads large masses, load distribution, structural vibration, and the balance of heavy movement. Where another traveler sees a wall, an Ɱʉɵ’Ŋþɨɍ may also feel how its weight is carried and where its weakness lies.

## 15 · ❄️ Ɨśƙǽɬ’Ʋǽɍʏþ
> *The Milk-Pale People of the Cold Biomes*

**Fantasy-reader bridge:** Ice-elf analogue  
**Common homelands:** Glacier valleys, frozen lakes, crystal snowfields, glass-ice palaces  
**Primary instinct:** **Thermal-Boundary Sense**

### Identity
Ɨśƙǽɬ’Ʋǽɍʏþ are recognizable by milk-pale skin, light hair, blue eyes, and pointed ears. Their homelands are not simply “the north.” They inhabit powerful cold A̤ɐ͜ɨǣ́ꞎ͡ƣ biomes scattered across the vast world.

Their bodies do more than survive extreme cold. They can direct it. Through close contact or breath, they can transmit an intense cold A̤ɐ͜ɨǣ́ꞎ͡ƣ effect as a serious and dangerous thermal attack.

### Culture
Control, restraint, and composure are major virtues. The ability to wield a force does not mean it should be used casually. Training therefore emphasizes discipline and environmental safety.

### Instinct and narrative identity
**Thermal-Boundary Sense** reads heat loss, freezing lines, and the movement of hot-cold boundaries. Ɨśƙǽɬ’Ʋǽɍʏþ embody a beautiful but dangerous force of nature refined into civilization.

## 16 · 🎭 Ƙɐꞎʏŧɨ’Ƴħɍǽ
> *The Form-Takers*

**Fantasy-reader bridge:** Shapeshifter / changeling analogue  
**Common homelands:** Road cities, workshops, architectural sites, dispersed communities  
**Primary instinct:** **Inner-Body / Essence Sense**  
**Guiding idea:** *Form changes.*

### Identity and form
Ƙɐꞎʏŧɨ’Ƴħɍǽ are known for blue-white complexions and visible energy markings. Their shapechanging is not a visual illusion. They can alter height, face, voice, proportions, hair, and outward form through genuine bodily transformation.

The foundation of this ability is their understanding of the body from **within**. To them, form is not a fixed shell. It is architecture that can be read and reorganized.

### Craft and civilization
Though often dispersed, they are renowned in architecture, talismanic craft, body knowledge, and A̤ɐ͜ɨǣ́ꞎ͡ƣ practice. They may appear in other peoples’ cities as masters, architects, advisers, or travelers.

### Instinct and narrative identity
**Inner-Body / Essence Sense** reads their own tissues, organs, proportions, forms, and active essences in extraordinary detail. Ƙɐꞎʏŧɨ’Ƴħɍǽ stories naturally explore identity, appearance, truth, and the question of what truly makes a person themselves.

## 17 · 🌸 Þǽ’Ɍʏŋ
> *The Two-Form People of the Flower Valleys*

**Fantasy-reader bridge:** Pixie / fae analogue  
**Common homelands:** Forests, springs, high trees, flower valleys, hidden hollows  
**Primary instinct:** **Air-Space Sense**  
**Guiding idea:** *The cycle continues.*

### Identity
Þǽ’Ɍʏŋ are graceful, nature-linked beings with two distinct forms. In their smaller form, tree hollows, leaf shelters, flowers, riverbank openings, and vine-hidden dwellings can become true homes rather than decorative hiding places.

They should not be mistaken for ornamental fairies. They possess their own communities, environmental knowledge, history, and territorial routes.

### Relationship with the world
Airflow, treetops, waterways, and the three-dimensional emptiness of a forest are part of their geography. A hedge or vertical woodland that looks impassable to another traveler may be full of routes to a Þǽ’Ɍʏŋ.

### Instinct and narrative identity
**Air-Space Sense** reads air currents, three-dimensional space, and natural passages. Their stories show how a small physical scale can still contain enormous adventure.

## 18 · 🐚 Ŋɇɍǽþ’Ʋǽꞎűŋɐ
> *The Solitary Pearl Hunters*

**Fantasy-reader bridge:** Aquatic serpentine folk with lamia and merfolk echoes  
**Common homelands:** Cave lakes, water tunnels, coastal caverns, root passages, freshwater routes

### Identity and body
Ŋɇɍǽþ’Ʋǽꞎűŋɐ are an all-female aquatic-serpentine people with extraordinarily long tails. The tail serves not only swimming, but climbing, gripping, defense, and the movement of heavy bodies through difficult terrain.

### Society
They favor personal territory and loose communities over crowded cities or rigid collective structures. Cave systems, waterways, hidden pools, and natural tunnels form the geography of daily life.

### Instinct and narrative identity
**Resonance-Emotion Sense** interprets how sound and vibration influence attention, emotion, and behavior. Combined with their powerful voices, it makes them masters of acoustic environments beneath stone and water.

# II · Stone, Storm, Root, and Cosmos

## 19 · ⛏️ Ǥɵɍűɱ’Ɱǽχɍʏþ
> *The Burrowers Who Renew the Underworld*

**Fantasy-reader bridge:** No direct equivalent; “subterranean ecosystem engineer” is the closest description  
**Common homelands:** Deep tunnels, mineral beds, clay basins, crystal regions  
**Primary instinct:** **Soil-Biome Sense**  
**Guiding idea:** *The world is not consumed; it is worked and enriched again.*

### Identity and ecological role
Ǥɵɍűɱ’Ɱǽχɍʏþ can consume stone, soil, and mineral matter. The process is more than feeding: through the interaction of their bodies, processed material, and Primordial A̤ɐ͜ɨǣ́ꞎ͡ƣ, they can alter underground environments over long spans of time.

A cave once inhabited by a Gorûm community may become a richer ecosystem centuries later. Natural A̤ɐ͜ɨǣ́ꞎ͡ƣ crystal beds are also specially associated with subterranean ecologies shaped by their presence.

### Culture
Good Gorûm tradition teaches not only how to dig, but **when to stop**. Over-mining and careless mineral consumption can destabilize a cave system, making ecological knowledge inseparable from craft.

### Instinct and narrative identity
**Soil-Biome Sense** reads stone and mineral condition, moisture, and the life-bearing potential of underground spaces. They make the relationship between mining, ecology, and civilization a direct part of worldbuilding.

## 20 · 🧲 Ʋɇƙþɐɍ’Ŋűɱʏɍ
> *The Engineers of Motion*

**Fantasy-reader bridge:** Weight and friction engineers  
**Common homelands:** Suspended cities, moving bridges, great shipyards, skyports  
**Primary instinct:** **Inertia-Vector Sense**  
**Guiding idea:** *Moving a thing is easy; mastery is stopping it in the right place.*

### The true nature of the gift
Ʋɇƙþɐɍ’Ŋűɱʏɍ can reduce effective weight and suppress surface friction for living or non-living targets. This is not unrestricted telekinetic flight. An object still requires a push, pull, wind, rope, machine, or other force to gain direction and speed.

That distinction defines their entire engineering tradition: **physics is not abolished; its conditions are altered.**

### Cities and safety
Their cities can move great stones, reposition bridges, and transport loads that would otherwise be impossible to handle. Yet misjudged momentum remains deadly. Safety procedures, planning, and calculation therefore carry cultural importance.

### Instinct and narrative identity
**Inertia-Vector Sense** reads momentum, friction, weight direction, and likely paths of continuation or stopping. Ʋɇƙþɐɍ’Ŋűɱʏɍ represent a world in which magic and engineering are partners rather than opposites.

## 21 · 🌩️ Þʏɍɐ’Ʋɇƙűɍʏŋ

For Þʏɍɐ’Ʋɇƙűɍʏŋ, the sky is not merely atmosphere. It is food, road, map, and life source. Their bodies live through electrical energy, making storm regions natural centers of civilization.

Nomadic fleets, balloon vessels, floating markets, and lightning-harvest stations separate their societies from land-bound kingdoms.

### Energy culture
Lightning can be harvested and stored in specialized vessels, powering individuals, ships, and settlements. Electrical attacks and storm effects are possible, but they draw from the same energy reserves that sustain life, ensuring that power always carries a cost.

### Instinct and narrative identity
**Electric-Field Sense** reads charge, lightning paths, and atmospheric electromagnetic change. Their stories are naturally suited to sky trade, energy scarcity, storm navigation, piracy, and mobile civilizations.

## 23 · 🌌 Ɐśŧɇɍʏŋ’Ʋɇʏɍχɐ
> *The Star-Woven People of the Cosmic Layers*

**Fantasy-reader bridge:** No direct equivalent; cosmic humanoid starfolk  
**Common homelands:** Outer atmosphere, cosmic layers, ring cities, observatories, stellar routes  
**Primary instinct:** **Cosmic-Layer Sense**  
**Guiding idea:** *The sky is not owned. It is read, loved, and traveled.*

### Identity
Ɐśŧɇɍʏŋ’Ʋɇʏɍχɐ are star-faring humanoids with natural lifespans measured in thousands of years. In ordinary life they use a calm, controllable movement state. When necessary, they can enter **ɐśŧɇɍ’ɍűŋ**, a second state in which perception, thought, reflex, bodily motion, and flight rise together to cosmic scales.

This state does not make them intangible or immortal. Their bodies become extraordinarily resilient, yet sufficiently powerful impacts or attacks can still injure or kill them.

### Cosmic scale
Within canonical setting physics, their upper movement scale can reach **1,000,000 light-years per minute**, allowing passage across the setting’s roughly 24-million-light-year atmospheric scale in about 24 minutes. These values belong to Ałek’ryŧhæ’s own cosmic physics rather than Old-Earth physical limits.

### Society and narrative identity
Observatories, ring cities, star-glass towers, route archives, and cosmic ports define their civilization. Exploration, navigation, astronomy, and affection for the sky matter more culturally than conquest.

**Cosmic-Layer Sense** reads massive bodies, stellar routes, layer boundaries, and cosmic direction. Ɐśŧɇɍʏŋ’Ʋɇʏɍχɐ expand fantasy’s horizon from kingdoms and continents to planets and star roads.

## 24 · 🐺 Ʋħɐṙǥǽþ’Ɍűŋ
> *The Werewolves of the Darkness Threshold*

**Fantasy-reader bridge:** Werewolf  
**Common environments:** Human cities, frontier towns, forest edges, night shelters, abandoned structures  
**Primary instinct:** **Transformation-Threshold Sense**  
**Guiding idea:** *Be alone before darkness falls.*

### Identity
Ʋħɐṙǥǽþ’Ɍűŋ are not a separate civilization born in hidden cities. They are a true werewolf transformation that occurs only in people of Ħǽɱ’Ŧʏǽɍŋ origin. By day they can live as ordinary humans, work, maintain homes, and remain visually indistinguishable from everyone around them.

Their transformation is **not tied to the full moon**. Canonically, it is triggered by the **Darkness Threshold**, the world’s transition into night.

### Werewolf state
The transformed body becomes a tall, heavily muscled, clawed, wolf-headed, charcoal-black predator. It is not entirely mindless, but it cannot reliably preserve the judgment of the human state. A tendency toward solitude rather than pack formation becomes an important form of night-time safety.

### Instinct and narrative identity
**Transformation-Threshold Sense** detects the approach of the transformation state and its resonance. The strongest Ʋħɐṙǥǽþ’Ɍűŋ stories are not simply about power. They are about responsibility, fear, the limits of self-control, and the burden of reorganizing one’s life to keep others safe.

# 🧬 Hybrids and Outer Realm Origins

JOA’s canonical framework begins with these 24 peoples, but the character system is not limited to pure canonical origins.

**True hybrids** may inherit multiple Primary Instinctive Roots from the canonical peoples carried within their lineage. One root does not automatically erase another; multiple perceptual channels may operate in the same body.

**Outer Realm** origins provide free-form space for characters designed beyond Ałek’ryŧhæ canon. This allows the canonical world to preserve its own laws while still welcoming characters from other universes or entirely original settings.
